Let the visual asymmetry do the talking. The left box is a single sentence. The right box is a shopping list. The footers carry the real punch: "1 sentence, 1 outcome" against "14+ commitments, 0 named customers." The point is not that the right side is wrong — it is that nobody asked for it yet.

Four problems, mixed into one

Four problems. Four clocks. One initiative.

These problems are not the same. Each should have its own track and milestones. Prematurely solving them in one architecture is how we end up with an unmaintainable system that nobody can operate.

Problem 1 · Clock: Jun 2027

Logi analytics replacement

A supported analytics path the day Logi turns off. Contractual. Non-negotiable. Named customers, named dashboards, fixed date.

Problem 2 · Clock: in flight

Customer self-service data access

Clients want to query Payment Hub data in their own BI tools. The datamart has gaps, but it exists today and already covers ~95% of analytics use cases.

Problem 3 · Clock: 2028+

A datalake for advanced & cross-product analytics

A new datalake may be needed for combined Payment Hub + Quantum analytics. Build, reuse, or interop. Zero named customers. Zero customer-validated use cases. 

Problem 4 · Clock: none yet

AI · ML · Anomaly detection · forecasting

Hot in RFPs and demos — nobody asking by name. Zero named customers. Zero labelled datasets. Zero customer-validated use cases. 

Each problem has its own clock, its own owner, its own evidence bar. Force the room to name which one they were just defending. The point of the four "clock" labels at the top of each panel is to make the timing asymmetry undeniable.

Horizon 2 · 2027 → 2028

Medium term:
Datalake; yes! Which use cases?

Validated requirements before architecture. Decide build-vs-reuse strategy and responsibilities with Quantum datalake. Only evolve our internal analytical schema where evidence - not enthusiasm - demands it.

Medium term · Four spikes, posed as questions

Let's not prematurely optimize. Answer 4 questions first.

Spike 01 · Evidence base

Which use cases justify investment?

Consolidate feedback from customers (Product Management), competitive analysis (Presales), look into internal initiatives like AF, Integrity datalake, etc. Rank. Each use case has a named customer and answers a real need.

Spike 02 · Thin slice

Is a simple Parquet export already enough?

Export the datamart tables to a Parquet file and store it on S3, partitioned by date. Validate with Quantum. Measure operational and maintenance costs. Before committing to our own solution.

Spike 03 · Quantum fit/gap

Build, reuse, integrate, or pause?

One choice, in writing, signed off between DEV and PM across both PH and Quantum. Two or more parallel data lake solutions are the worst outcome vis-a-vis the competition.

Spike 04 · Minimum viable analytics model

What does the smallest defensible analytical schema/model look like?

Every new fact and dimension must cite a customer-validated report it enables, and/or a gap it closes.

Each spike produces a decision artefact, not a doc. The Parquet thin slice in particular is "built code that we may not extend" — that is healthy engineering, not waste.

Medium term · The thin(nest) slice

The most useful thing to build may be the smallest.

The simplest viable path

datamart  →  ETL  →  Parquet on S3
                       (partitioned by date)
                              ↓
                  SQL engine of customer's choice

No Iceberg. No Bronze/Silver/Gold medallion ETL process. No data catalogue. Validate with Quantum Data Hub, Athena, Power BI, QuickSight ...

After the thin Parquet export, Explicitly choose one:

  1. The export is enough - ship it. 90% of the room is relieved.
  2. Align with Quantum. Reuse what's there. Or learn and pivot.
  3. Build a dedicated lake (Horizon 3), with evidence in hand.

The answer may be just providing a Parquet export. We will find out by prioritizing building one.

The dry punchline: the simplest possible answer might be the entire answer. That is not a failure of ambition — that is a successful piece of evidence.

Horizon 3 · 2028+

Long term:
only if justified.

An advanced data platform we may one day operate. Beware complexity and maintainability!

Long term · use cases required

An advanced data analytics platform / DataOps / MLOps

Six gates. Every commitment on this data platform should pass all six or it doesn't ship to production. 

01

Customer validation

Named customers, known inputs, known expected outputs and side-effects, customer-validated end-to-end scenarios and outcomes.

02

Simplest viable alternative

Show us why direct datamart access or a simple Parquet export is insufficient. Use case documentation required.

03

Maintainability

At least 5 engineers not involved in the design and architecture can fully explain, support and change it.

04

Operational ownership

CIO, PS, CX, Security, Product Management, Development: explicit, named, and signed off.

05

Cost & commercials

Simple per-customer cost closely tied to pricing or a funding line. No magic proposals.

06

Data freshness honesty

Batch / hourly vs near-real-time / synchronous data use cases are strictly separated. Every real-time requirement, needs a named reason.

The point of the six gates is to make "no" easy to say without sounding like a no-person.

Long term · AI · ML · anomaly detection

Discovery, not delivery.

This is currently too vague and unactionable. This is honesty; not a lack of ambition.

What we will do

  • Start with one use case, clearly defined end-to-end (anything).
  • Replicate our statistical fraud detection first; the product already has SQL-driven fraud patterns.
  • Define the workflow, actions, false-positive plan, explainability, audit, owners.
  • Scope a governed LLM / MCP spike against synthetic data.

What we will NOT do

  • Build AWS SageMaker pipelines / Feature data stores / model-serving infra.
  • Deploy CDC streaming for sub-10 ms scoring.
  • Wire AWS Bedrock inference into production payments workflows.
  • Promise any unfounded AI capability to a customer, an RFP, or a sales deck.
“AI/ML” is downstream of trustworthy data — not the foundation for it.
If anyone pushes for more detail here, ask them: which named customer or RFP needs this in 2026? That single question is the entire test.

The ask · Four decisions, today

What we all need to agree on today.

Decision 01

Split up the current all-in-one datalake initiative

Clear distinction between Logi replacement, datalake evolution, and AI/ML use cases. Separate scope, owners, funding, and cadence (mirroring our internal capacity).

Decision 02

Freeze any solutioning that already happened.

Useful as long-term reference material, but not in the next sprint. No new build under it until the discussed gates pass.

Decision 03

Focus on Logi replacement via self-service data access for analytics

Read-only datamart tables access, external BI tooling POC, runbook, pilots, improved synthetic dataset.

Decision 04

Close collaboration on new business case documents for anything beyond Logi replacement

Product Management (customer-validated use cases), Presales (prospects/competition), PS (existing projects) and Development (product feasibility) to work together on new business cases.

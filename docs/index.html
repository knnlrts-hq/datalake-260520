The current gap · Ask vs. answer

High-level requirements versus low-level technical architecture.

What the 06/25 business case asks for:

“Export Payment Hub data (on demand or scheduled) so clients can build reports in their own tools, eventually combinable with Quantum.”

That is the entire document.

What the 05/26 architecture answers with:

Per-customer AWS lakehouses · Apache Iceberg · Bronze / Silver / Gold medallion architecture · Lake Formation governance · SageMaker ML pipelines · real-time sub-5 ms anomaly scoring · Bedrock LLM explanations · CDC streaming · sub-10 ms inference · cash forecasting · cost tiering · DR · IAM · observability.

These things are two to three orders of magnitude apart - but who's counting...

Let the visual asymmetry do the talking. The left box is a single sentence. The right box is a shopping list. The point is not that the right side is wrong — it is that nobody asked for it yet.

Four problems, rolled into one budget

We are mixing different things into one initiative.

These things are not the same. Solving these four problems in one architecture is exactly how we will end up with an unmaintainable system that nobody can operate in the future.

Problem 1 · Deadline Jun 2027

Logi license replacement

Customers need a supported analytics path the day Logi turns off. Contractual. Non-negotiable.

Problem 2 · In-flight

Customer self-service data access

Clients want to query Payment Hub data with their own BI tools. The datamart has gaps, however it exists today and already covers 95% of analytics use cases.

Problem 3 · Strategic

A datalake platform for advanced analytics

A new datalake may be needed for cross-product analytics and Quantum alignment. 

Problem 4 · Speculative

AI · ML · Anomaly detection · forecasting

Candidate capabilities. Zero validated customer-driven use cases. Zero labelled training datasets. Hot topic in RFPs and with prospects, but zero customers asking by name.

Each problem has its own clock, its own owner, its own evidence bar. Force the room to name which one they were just defending.

DEV + PS + OPS · 4-8 wks

Short term · What is decidedly out of scope

Things we will not build.
A partial list.

  • An Apache Iceberg lakehouse
  • A Bronze / Silver / Gold medallion ETL process
  • CDC streaming or Flink
  • AWS SageMaker feature dataset stores
  • A new analytical schema
  • AWS Bedrock LLM explanations
  • Sub-10 ms inference
  • Cash forecasting models
  • Per-customer AWS stacks
  • New analytics dashboards

None of these have any named customer asking for it... Do they?

The "partial list" subtitle is the joke. Don't oversell it. The list is the argument: if nobody on the customer side is asking for any of this on the deadline horizon, none of it belongs on the deadline horizon.

Short term

Definition of done.

One customer in production on the new analytics path — with a runbook a non-developer can execute, against a datamart that has zero write paths from the consumer side.

Before June 2027. Everything else can wait.

2 named pilots: Atlas Copco, UNICC
1 BI tool, end-to-end, before adding a second
0 write paths to the operational DB
The room will ask "how do we know we're done?" — this slide is the answer, written down. The "1 BI tool" line is the one that prevents scope creep in March 2027 when somebody decides Power BI is also a hill we should die on.

Horizon 2 · 2027 → 2028

Medium term:
Datalake; yes! Which use cases?

Requirements before architecture. Decide build-vs-reuse strategy and responsibilities with Quantum. Evolve the analytical schema where evidence - not enthusiasm - demands it.

Medium term · Four spikes, posed as questions

Let's not prematurely optimize.
Answer four questions first.

Spike 01 · Evidence base

Which use cases justify investment?

Consolidate feedback from customers (product managemen), competitve analysis (presales), look into internal intiatives like AF, Integrity datalake, etc. Rank. Each use case has a customer name and answer a need.

Spike 02 · Thin slice

Is a simple Parquet export enough?

Export the datamart tables to a Parquet file and store it on S3, partitioned by date. Validate with Quantum. Measure cost. Before committing to our own solution.

Spike 03 · Quantum fit/gap

Build, reuse, integrate, or pause?

One choice, in writing, signed off between dev and PM across both PH and Quantum. Two or more parallel data lake solutions are the worst outcome.

Spike 04 · Minimum viable analytics model

What does the smallest defensible model look like?

Every new fact and dimension cites: a customer report it enables, and a gap it closes.

Each spike produces a decision artefact, not a doc. The Parquet thin slice in particular is "built code that we may not extend" — that is healthy engineering, not waste.

Medium term · The thin(nest) slice

The most useful thing we can build may also be the smallest.

The simplest viable path

datamart  →  ETL  →  Parquet on S3
                       (partitioned by date)
                              ↓
                  SQL engine of customer's choice

No Iceberg. No Bronze/Silver/Gold medallion. No data catalogue. Validate with Quantum Data Hub, Athena, Power BI, QuickSight ...

After the thin slice, Explicitly choose one:

  1. The export is enough - ship it. 90% of the room is relieved.
  2. Align with Quantum. Reuse what's there.
  3. Build a dedicated lake (Horizon 3), with evidence in hand.

The answer may be just providing a Parquet export. We will find out by building one.

The dry punchline: the simplest possible answer might be the entire answer. That is not a failure of ambition — that is a successful piece of evidence.

Medium term · Done when

Horizon 2 closes when we can answer four questions in writing.

01

Which use cases justify investment?

Ranked, named, agreed, owned.

02

Is the Parquet export enough?

Thin slice shipped. Real customer feedback in hand.

03

Build, reuse, or integrate with Quantum?

One option chosen, dated, signed by both Development Architects and Product Management.

04

What is the minimum viable analytical model?

Customer-validated. Backward compatibility documented. Not exhaustive: minimum.

These four answers earn Horizon 3. Anything less should not force it.

The closing line is the load-bearing one. Horizon 3 is not on a calendar — it is on a gate.

Horizon 3 · 2028+

Long term:
only if justified.

The advanced data platform we may one day operate. 

Long term · Receipts required

The datalake solution we may one day operate.

Six gates. Every commitment on this platform should pass all six or it doesn't ship to production. 

01

Customer validation

Named customers, known inputs, known expected outputs and side-effects, documented end-to-end scenarios.

02

Simplest viable alternative

Show us why direct datamart access or Parquet export is insufficient. Use case documentation required.

03

Maintainability

At least 5 engineers not involved in the design can explain, support and change it.

04

Operational ownership

OPS, PS, CX, Security, Product Magament, Development -  explicit, named, signed.

05

Cost & commercial

Simple per-customer cost closely tied to pricing or a funding line. No magic proposals.

06

Freshness honesty

Batch / hourly / near-real-time / synchronous are strictly separated. Every real-time requirement, needs a named reason.

Promising demos do not count

The stamp at the bottom-right is the only humour cue on this slide. Let it sit. The point of the six gates is to make "no" easy to say without sounding like a no-person.

Long term · AI · ML · anomaly detection

Discovery, not delivery.

This is currently the vaguest section. This is honesty; not a lack of ambition.

What we will do

  • Pick one use case, clearly defined end-to-end (anything).
  • Try a statistical baseline first; the product already has SQL fraud patterns.
  • Define action, false-positive plan, explainability, audit, owner.
  • Scope a governed LLM / MCP spike against synthetic data.

What we will not do

  • Build AWS SageMaker pipelines / Feature data stores / model-serving infra.
  • Deploy CDC streaming for sub-10 ms scoring.
  • Wire Bedrock into production payments workflows.
  • Promise any unfounded AI capability to a customer, an RFP, or a sales deck.
“AI/ML” is downstream of trustworthy data — not the foundation for it.
If anyone pushes for more detail here, ask them: which named customer or RFP needs this in 2026? That single question is the entire test.

The ask · Four decisions, today

What we need to agree on today.

Decision 01

Split up the datalake initiative

Clear disctinction between Logi replacement, data lake evolution, AI/ML use cases. Separate scope, owners, funding, cadence.

Decision 02

Freeze any solutioning that already happened.

Long-term reference material, not the next sprint. No new build under it until gates pass.

Decision 03

Focus on Logi replacement

Read-only datamart tables access, BI tooling POC, runbook, pilots, synthetic dataset.

Decision 04

Collaborate on a new business case document for anything beyond Logi replacement

Product Management (customer use cases) and Development (feasibility) to work together on a new business case.

We are not anti-platform. We are pro-customer, pro-deadline, and pro-team-that-can-maintain-the-thing-they-built. — the bottom line.
Read the four decisions slowly. Look at the relevant leader for each one. Then read the quote. Don't add new arguments — by this slide the argument is over and we are collecting signatures.

Closing

Focus on existing customers first.
Focus on clear use cases.
Then build the platform.

Tactical delivery now · discovery in parallel · platform investment only when the evidence demands it · AI/ML when the data is ready to support it.

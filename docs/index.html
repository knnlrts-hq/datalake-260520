Trax Analytics  ·  Internal Alignment

First keep customers whole.
Then earn the right to build the platform.

Resetting the Trax Analytics & Data Lake initiative into a sequenced, evidence-driven delivery plan that protects the Logi deadline and the architecture both.

Koen Naulaerts For PM · PS · Presales · Dev May 2026
Open by anchoring the room: the goal of this talk is shared understanding, not winning an argument. I want PM, PS, Presales and Dev to leave with the same mental model of what we are solving now and what we are deliberately not solving yet. The thesis is two sentences on the slide. Read them. Pause. Then move on.

Where we are

One deadline. One ambitious plan. Four functions with different mental models.

The clock

Logi sunset

Logi license ends 27 June 2026. Customers need a supported way to keep their analytics running. This is the only piece of the initiative with a hard, dated, contractual constraint.

The plan on the table

A 5-phase platform

Per-customer AWS lakehouse, Iceberg, Bronze/Silver/Gold, SageMaker, anomaly detection, Bedrock explanations, CDC streaming, sub-10 ms scoring, cash forecasting. Technically coherent. Long-term valuable.

The room

Four lenses

PM sees roadmap risk. PS sees customer commitments. Presales sees RFP narrative. Dev sees a system the current team cannot yet operate.

We are about to make a sequencing decision. Today is about making it together.

Set the stakes without pointing fingers. The Logi date is real and external. The architecture is real and on the table. The four lenses are real and different. None of these is wrong; they are just unaligned. The point of this talk is to align them.

Framing

What this talk is — and what it is not.

It is

  • A conversation about sequencing.
  • A proposal to split one initiative into three tracks with different cadences.
  • A request for decision gates before we commit to a multi-year platform build.
  • A way to protect the Logi deadline and the architecture work both.
NOT

It is not

  • A critique of the architecture design. It is solid reference material.
  • A claim that we will never build a data lake. We probably will.
  • A push for cheap over good. It is a push for earned complexity.
  • A vote against AI/ML. It is a vote for doing AI/ML well, when the data is ready.
This slide exists to lower defenses. Read both columns out loud. The architecture work stays alive as long-term reference; what we are challenging is when and how we commit to building it.

Problem 1  /  Urgent + Validated

Logi continuity.

The constraint

  • Logi license sunset 27 June 2026.
  • Multiple customers depend on Logi-based analytics today.
  • Existing dashboards, queries and dependencies are well understood.
  • Pilot candidates already named: Atlas Copco, UNICC.

What success looks like

  • Customers can keep doing today’s reporting through a supported path.
  • PS/CX has a repeatable runbook, not a hero project.
  • Dashboards reproducible in at least one BI tool.
  • No write access to the operational database.

This is the only piece of the initiative that fails if we miss the date.

Use this slide to anchor urgency. Everything downstream — AI, platform, data lake — can be re-debated next quarter. The Logi date cannot.

Problem 2  /  Strategic + Validated

Customer self-service access to Payment Hub data.

“Provide a flexible way for clients to access or export Payment Hub data — on demand or scheduled — so they can build reports in their own tools, including eventual combined reporting with Quantum data.”

— Business case, paraphrased

What it asks

Access. Export. Self-service. Combinable with Quantum.

What it doesn’t ask

Iceberg. ML. Real-time. Per-customer lakehouse. Anomaly detection.

Implication

A read-only datamart path — with optional Parquet export — already satisfies it.

This is the slide where I want everyone to feel a small “oh.” The business case is small. The proposed solution is large. That gap is the entire reason we are having this conversation.

Problem 3  /  Strategic + Exploratory

A future-state analytical platform.

The motivation is real:

  • OLTP querying is slow and risky.
  • The existing datamart has known gaps — lifecycle, approvals, governance, counterparty, statements, client extensions.
  • LVMH, Allianz, SLB reports bypass the datamart and hit the operational DB directly.
  • Quantum / Treasury Data Hub alignment may unlock cross-product reporting.

But the validation isn’t there yet:

  • No ranked, customer-attributed use-case list.
  • No agreed freshness classification (batch vs hourly vs near-real-time).
  • No build-vs-reuse-vs-integrate decision with Quantum.
  • No measured cost or commercial model.

The right move is discovery first, then a thin slice, then — only if justified — a platform.

Notice the careful framing. Yes, the platform may be needed. No, we don’t know enough yet to commit to building it. Discovery is not avoidance — it is the responsible next step.

Problem 4  /  Speculative

AI / ML / Anomaly detection / MCP.

These are candidate capabilities, not requirements. Before any of them earns a roadmap slot, each must answer:

Decision

  • What action does a positive signal trigger?
  • Who owns the action?

Data

  • What labels exist?
  • What feature dataset?
  • Real-time or batch?

Governance

  • Explainability for compliance.
  • False-positive handling.
  • PII boundaries.

“AI/ML” is downstream of trustworthy data — not the foundation for it.

The temptation here is to demote AI. Resist it. Don’t demote — sequence. AI/ML gets built when the data, labels, governance and operational loops are real. We are simply naming what “real” means.

The gap

One paragraph of requirement. Five phases of platform.

Business case

“Export any data. Build reports in client tools. Combine Trax + Quantum. On demand or scheduled.”

No defined users. No concrete use cases. No volumes. No latency targets. No acceptance criteria. No commercial model.

GAP

Act II  /  02

Why sequencing
matters more
than ambition.

Most data platforms don’t fail on design. They fail on the order in which they were built.

Transition from problem-space (Act I) to argument (Act II). The point of this act is to make sequencing — not architecture — the right axis to argue on.

Credit where credit is due

The proposed architecture is good reference material.

The design contains decisions worth keeping as long-term reference, even if we don’t build them this year:

Architectural ideas worth banking

  • Physical separation of analytics from OLTP.
  • Read-replica + JDBC ingestion pattern.
  • Open formats (Parquet, optionally Iceberg).
  • Bronze/Silver/Gold layering as a mental model.
  • VPC endpoints, TLS, SSE-KMS, masking, row-level security.

Product ideas worth banking

  • The analytical use-case catalogue (volumes, FX, rejections, dwell time, cutoffs).
  • Statement-level facts and lifecycle modelling.
  • The principle: every schema traces back to a documented consumer need.
  • Quantum/Treasury Data Hub alignment as a strategic option.

We are not throwing this away. We are putting it in the right order.

Diplomatic but sincere. The architecture has real value as a north star. Saying so on the record protects the working relationship while still making the sequencing argument.

Five guardrails

Rules of engagement for everything that follows.

01

Customer need before architecture.

Every epic names a customer, RFP, or contractual need. If we cannot, we don’t start it.

02

Tactical continuity is not blocked by strategic ambition.

Logi replacement must not wait for a data lake to exist.

03

Batch-first unless real-time is proven.

Daily batch is 8–10× cheaper than hourly. Streaming is a separate product, not a default.

04

No AI/ML without a feature dataset and a decision loop.

Labels, features, action path, false-positive handling, governance — or we don’t build a model.

05

Maintainability is a release criterion.

If a second engineer can’t explain, operate and evolve it, it isn’t an asset — it’s future debt.

These five sit above any specific epic. They are how we judge whether something belongs on the next sprint board. Read them out. Repeat #05 — it’s the one that most quietly fails in agent-generated architectures.

If we don’t sequence

The predictable failure mode.

Month 1–3

Architecture churn. Diagrams. AWS meetings. No customer-facing delivery.

Month 3–6

Logi deadline approaches. PS scrambles. Pilots delayed.

Month 6–9

Deadline missed or barely met with workarounds. Customer confidence dips.

Month 9–12

Partial platform exists. Unclear ownership. Difficult to operate.

Year 2

ML system without trusted labels. Silent rollback to simpler tooling.

The risk is not that the architecture is wrong. The risk is that we deliver none of it while missing the one deadline we cannot move.

This is the “cost of inaction” slide. Not melodramatic — this is the textbook failure mode for over-scoped data platforms. Naming it removes its power.

Track 1  /  What “done” looks like

Concrete acceptance criteria across the five epics.

Access & Reproduction

  • At least one internal env queries DM read-only via the agreed access path.
  • Standard query pack reproduces representative Logi dashboards in Superset.
  • Gaps classified: missing data · BI visualization · performance · client customization · unsupported.
  • Zero write access to the operational DB.

Operations & Pilots

  • Runbook covers prerequisites, approvals, security, validation, rollback, support handoff.
  • Version compatibility matrix published for in-scope releases.
  • Atlas Copco & UNICC complete UAT and run the standard query pack.
  • Pilot feedback drives a documented “go / hold / cancel” decision for wider rollout.

Four epics that earn the right to platform investment.

E5

Product + BA

Architecture

Analytical model evolution

The discovery work is real engineering, not paper-pushing. Each of these epics produces a decision artefact, not just a doc. The Parquet thin slice in particular is “built code that we may not extend” — that’s healthy.

Track 3  /  AI · ML · MCP

Discovery only. No platform commitments. Yet.

What we will do:

  • Pick one anomaly use case (e.g. unusual payment / beneficiary).
  • Define labels, features, action path, false-positive handling.
  • Compare a statistical baseline against an ML approach.
  • Scope a governed MCP / LLM access spike against synthetic data.

What we will not do:

  • Build SageMaker / Feature Store / model-serving infra.
  • Deploy CDC streaming for sub-10 ms scoring.
  • Wire Bedrock explanations into production paths.
  • Promise any AI capability to customers, RFPs or sales decks.

The long term gets vaguer on purpose. That is honesty, not a lack of ambition.

The deliberate vagueness here is a feature. If anyone pushes for more detail, ask them: which named customer or RFP needs this in 2026? That question is the entire test.

Unified roadmap

Phased — with decision gates between each horizon.

Phase 1 · now → Q3

Stabilize & deliver

DM read-only access · Superset POC · runbook · pilots (Atlas Copco, UNICC) · synthetic dataset.

Phase 2 · Q3 → Q4

Validate direction

Use-case catalogue · Parquet thin slice · Quantum alignment spike · minimum viable analytical model.

Phase 3 · 2027

Build only what’s justified

Lake MVP if — and only if — Phase 2 evidence demands it. Smallest durable platform.

Phase 4 · beyond

Earned advanced analytics

Anomaly detection (statistical first, ML if proven) · governed MCP · forecasting. Each opens only on its own gate.

Between each phase: a decision-gate review. Phases do not auto-extend.

The diamonds between phases are the most important visual on this slide. Every phase is exit-gated. Nothing extends by default.

The ask, by function

Each group owns one decision and one deliverable.

Product Management

Owns: the ranked use-case catalogue (E5) and the “build / defer / reject” call on every Track 2 epic.

By end of Phase 1: a customer-attributed top-10 use case list with freshness classification.

Professional Services / CX

Owns: the pilot rollout for Atlas Copco & UNICC, and the support model post-pilot.

By end of Phase 1: an executable client-readiness checklist and a documented support-handoff path.

Presales

Owns: the RFP narrative consistency — what we promise externally must match what we are building.

By end of Phase 1: updated talking points for “analytics after Logi” that don’t over-commit on AI/ML.

Engineering / Architecture

Owns: Track 1 delivery, the Parquet thin slice (E6), and the Quantum alignment spike (E7).

By end of Phase 1: a working DM access path, reproduced dashboards, and a Quantum fit/gap document.

Horizon 2 · 2027 → 2028

Medium term:
earn the platform.

Requirements before architecture. Decide build-vs-reuse with Quantum. Evolve the model where evidence — not enthusiasm — demands it.

Medium term · Four spikes, posed as questions

Don't build a platform.
Answer four questions first.

Spike 05 · Evidence base

Which use cases justify investment?

Consolidate Logi, LVMH/Allianz/SLB, Quantum, SCN, anomaly, Integrity. Rank. Each entry names a customer, a question, an owner, a freshness class.

Spike 06 · Thin slice

Is a Parquet export enough?

Export the datamart to S3 Parquet, partitioned by date. Validate with Athena, Power BI, Superset. Measure cost. Before committing to Iceberg.

Spike 07 · Quantum fit/gap

Build, reuse, or integrate?

One choice, in writing, signed: reuse Quantum · integrate via Parquet handoff · build PHUB-specific · or pause. Two parallel platforms is the worst outcome.

Spike 08 · Minimum viable model

What does the smallest defensible model look like?

Every new fact and dimension cites: a customer report it enables, a gap it closes, an SME who signs the source-to-target.

Each spike produces a decision artefact, not a doc. The Parquet thin slice in particular is "built code that we may not extend" — that is healthy engineering, not waste.

Medium term · The thin slice

The most useful thing we can build right now
might also be the smallest.

The simplest viable path

datamart  →  ETL  →  Parquet on S3
                       (partitioned by date)
                              ↓
                  SQL engine of customer's choice

No Iceberg. No Bronze/Silver/Gold. No catalogue ceremony. Validate with Quantum DH, Athena, Power BI, QuickSight, Superset.

After the thin slice, choose one

  1. The export is enough — ship it. Half the room is quietly relieved.
  2. Align with Quantum. Reuse what's there.
  3. Build a dedicated lake (Horizon 3), with evidence in hand.

Maybe the answer was always a Parquet export. We will find out by building one.

The dry punchline: the simplest possible answer might be the entire answer. That is not a failure of ambition — that is a successful piece of evidence.

Long term · Receipts required

The lake we may one day deserve.

Six gates. Every platform commitment passes all six — or it doesn't ship. No exceptions, including for promising demos.

01

Customer validation

Named customers, named reports, documented end-to-end scenarios.

02

Simplest viable alternative

Show why DM access or Parquet export is insufficient. Receipts required.

03

Maintainability

Two engineers not involved in design can explain, support and change it.

04

Operational ownership

OPS, PS/CX, Security, Product, Engineering — explicit, named, signed.

05

Cost & commercial

Per-customer cost tied to pricing or a funding line. No magic.

06

Freshness honesty

Batch / hourly / near-real-time / synchronous separated. Real-time needs a named reason.

Promising demos do not count

The stamp at the bottom-right is the only humour cue on this slide. Let it sit. The point of the six gates is to make "no" easy to say without sounding like a no-person.

Long term · AI · ML · MCP

Discovery, not delivery.

The vaguest section in the deck. That is honesty — not a lack of ambition.

What we will do

  • Pick one anomaly use case (unusual amount, beneficiary, country, or timing).
  • Try a statistical baseline first — the product already has SQL fraud patterns.
  • Define action, false-positive plan, explainability, audit, owner.
  • Scope a governed MCP / LLM spike against synthetic data.

What we will not do

  • Build SageMaker / Feature Store / model-serving infra.
  • Deploy CDC streaming for sub-10 ms scoring.
  • Wire Bedrock into production paths.
  • Promise any AI capability to a customer, an RFP, or a sales deck.
“AI/ML” is downstream of trustworthy data — not the foundation for it.
If anyone pushes for more detail here, ask them: which named customer or RFP needs this in 2026? That single question is the entire test.

The ask · Four decisions, today

What we need from this room.

Decision 01

Split the initiative into three tracks.

Logi replacement, platform evolution, AI/ML. Separate scope, owners, funding, cadence.

Decision 02

Freeze the 5-phase architecture as reference.

Long-term reference material, not the next sprint. No new build under it until gates pass.

Decision 03

Fund steps 1–4 + 12. This quarter.

Read-only DM access, BI POC, runbook, pilots, synthetic dataset. Named owners. Real dates.

Decision 04

Adopt the six gates as policy.

Nothing crosses from Horizon 2 to Horizon 3 without all six. No exceptions, including for promising demos.

We are not anti-platform. We are pro-customer, pro-deadline, and pro-team-that-can-maintain-the-thing-they-built. — the bottom line.
